Multi-Period Tree Example

SYNTHETIC – NOT ADIC DATA

A small, fully solved version of the optimizer: 3 years × 3 regimes = 40 nodes. One linear program (559 variables × 816 constraints) decides every node at once. Solver: HiGHS 1.15.1 dual simplex via highspy (basic solution, exact duals). Independently re-solved with a second solver (CLARABEL) with matching results.

Case: Normal PE drought Crisis + oil shock Edge labels = probability of that branch · λ = liquidity premium (value of $1 of extra cash at that node) · dashed purple outline = decision differs between the two cases

How this works

  1. The tree. Start today in a PE drought. Each year the economy moves to Normal, Drought or Crisis with the probabilities on the edges (a crisis comes with an oil shock: the owner draws 10% of NAV, the credit line is only 50% available, and PE sells at a 35% discount).
  2. Cash must balance at every box. In: PE distributions, yield-book cash, interest. Out: the owner's draw (always paid in full), PE capital calls, interest on borrowing.
  3. At every box the optimizer chooses SELL / HOLD / REFINANCE / LEVER for each book, credit-line use and PE pacing, knowing only the path so far (it never sees which branch comes next).
  4. All boxes are decided together by one optimization: maximize the expected risk-averse value of the 3-year outcome, subject to the cash floor, borrowing limits and leverage cap. Same inputs, same answer.
  5. Only today's decision is executed. Next quarter the tree is rebuilt from where ADIC actually is and solved again (rolling re-optimization).
  6. λ, the liquidity price, is the value of one more dollar of cash at that box. It is what Corporate Finance would charge as its transfer price, and what decides SELL vs HOLD for a fund: sell only if the bid beats the value of holding.

The production design is the same mechanism at larger scale: quarterly steps, about 576 end-states over 8 periods, six model worlds and all eight groups, re-solved every quarter. Probabilities for the IC are then certified on 10,000–50,000 simulated paths. Humans set the limits and approve; no AI produces any number here.
