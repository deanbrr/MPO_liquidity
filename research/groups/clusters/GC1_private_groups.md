## 1. Executive summary

1. **One engine, five instrument sets.** The groups differ in instruments, control rights and forecast biases, not in mathematics. Fund interests run on C2's RFT-TA v2; single companies (co-investments, continuation-vehicle (CV) stakes, Capital Solutions kickers, PE look-through) run on one enterprise-value engine with one draw per company per path; contractual claims (Capital Solutions debt and preferred, private-credit loans) run on one competing-risks credit engine. Every action is scored with C2's deflator and full reduced cost; group P&L is attribution only.

2. **Control rights decide which actions exist.** CV elections and recaps are GP-triggered; closed-end recycling is the GP's; delayed-draw funding is contractual; tertiary sales, strips and pledges need GP consent and ROFR waivers; portfolio facilities belong to CF. Every action carries a feasibility flag, a consent probability and a lead time, and GC2 gate V23 rejects LP-inaccessible instruments.

3. **No private group hedges the cash channel of the DPI drought.**
   - **PE** distributes 9% of NAV a year (H1-2026) against 17–24% in 2017–20, while calls hold at 27–31% [S].
   - **Secondary funds** lose most of their DPI edge: DPI reaches 1.0× in year 8 rather than 6 [I-calc].
   - **Capital Solutions'** cash moves *with* PE distributions (NAV loans repay from distributions; preferreds switch to PIK). The direction is robust; the dossier's +0.96 correlation is not reproducible [C].
   - **Co-investment's** year-1 distribution yield is 12.4% from a drought start, against 19.3% from normal [I-calc].
   - **Private Credit's** SMA run-off is the best natural DPI (≈22% of SMA NAV a year now), but only the SMA sleeve can switch: ≈$0.8–0.9bn in year 1 (5–10% of the payout), not $2.2bn [C].

4. **Discounts are not value.** Buying an LP interest at 87% of NAV books +14.9% on day one under US GAAP, but the economic gain is (θ − p)N and tail-end θ is ≈0.80–0.83 [C]: tail-end discounts mostly pay for overstated NAV. The G4 buyer model bids an average co-investment at 71–79% of NAV in normal markets and 56–65% in a drought [C]; "87% of single-asset CV volume above 90% of NAV" describes completed trophy deals.

5. **Pacing is the PE group's main lever. It is slow, and it is not "10.5–11% financing"**, which restates the engine's growth assumption [C]. A cut costs forgone net α*, GP access, vintage diversification and co-investment flow, priced with the kernel; the forgone beta can be rebuilt through the overlay. A one-year pause returns ≈20% of the forgone commitment in year 1 and ≈40% (ERSRI) to 69% (engine) by year 4, then costs cash in years 6–14 [S/I-calc]. Steer exposure (unfunded + NAV) by MPC inside a stage-dependent corridor, protect a core-franchise sleeve, and never default on a call.

6. **Asset-level leverage rarely survives losses, PIK and path-wise covenants.** Alternative-lender back-leverage of the Capital Solutions book lowers loss-adjusted RoE (8.9% vs 9.25% unlevered) and carries −2.3% cash per dollar of debt at drought PIK levels [C]. A 12% holdco PIK on a ≈9% co-investment adds no return and lifts the at-exit wipe-out probability from 2.1% to 11.8% (normal) and from 7.0% to 22.8% (drought) [C]. Single-asset NAV facilities breach a 1.5× LTV trigger on 50–63% of paths when tested quarterly [C], and on 62–73% if LTV is monitored continuously [I-calc]. The corrected Broadie–Glasserman–Kou barrier shift changes neither result [re-checked 2026-10-18: `research/groups/addenda/G4_barrier_rerun/RESULTS.md` §5]. Private-credit back-leverage belongs only on senior, non-software SMA books in benign or normal states, at ≤40–45% starting LTV. This matches C2 D13.

7. **Counter-cyclical deployment is a franchise hedge, priced by ℓ.** Break-even IRR ≈ (1 + r_h)(1 + ℓ̄) − 1, with r_h the risk-matched return and ℓ̄ averaged over the capital outstanding. Under stream 08's 9/9/4 fallback, four-year structured equity must clear ≈13.3% and a drought-regime senior NAV loan ≈14.9%; at a dual near GC2's synthetic 1.83%, ≈8.3% and ≈8.1% [I-calc]. The G3 dossier and its verifier discounted at r_f, which understates the bar. Hold no idle reserve at the fallback ℓ; fund opportunity budgets from low-ℓ sources.

8. **Internal transfers raise no liquidity, and in-house quotes are evidence, not prices.** The Secondaries group publishes scored forecasts of *external* clearing prices but never the reference quote on a deal it may bid for. Capital Solutions' quote function, re-anchored to LP-level comparables (≈SOFR+300bp, LTV 10–20%), sets a reserve price behind third-party RFQs.

9. **Forecasts enter one engine with group-specific bias covariates:** PE timing slips of 2–8 quarters on top of level optimism; Secondaries booking discounts as alpha; Capital Solutions treating contractual yield as expected return; Co-investment assuming short holds, multiple expansion and leverage above 6×; Private Credit counting PIK as cash and using syndicated-loan recoveries. GC2's shared priors apply after gross-to-net conversion, with one precision weight and no extra credibility multiplier.

10. **The load-bearing unknowns are ADIC's:** PE unfunded by vintage, which decides whether 2026 PE net cash flow is +$0.2bn (mature program) or −$5 to −7bn (fast-growing) [I-calc]; the private-credit wrapper mix and SMA rights, which size the run-off lever; and LPA consent terms, which decide which SELL and REFINANCE actions exist.

## 4. Component specifications

Every position carries `group`, `is_synthetic`, look-through company and GP ids, consent/ROFR/pledge flags, bitemporal u/a/f series and an encumbrance flag. Every action is scored by C2 §3.3; LAPV is the liquidity-adjusted present value under the C2 deflator. The per-group rules instantiate C2 §4.4: **SELL** iff $p^{ceq}>p^*=[\mathbb E\,\mathrm{LAPV}(\text{HOLD}+\text{best REFI})+\kappa^{GP}+\kappa^{irr}-\text{relief}]/NAV+c$; **REFI** iff $s_B+f/T+\lambda_{Lev}+$covenant charge $<\bar\ell_{[0,T]}$; **ADD** iff $\alpha^*-\bar\ell_{[0,D]}-\lambda_Rm+(1-p^{buy})/D>0$; **LEVER** iff ADD and REFI both hold.
