## 2. Core concepts and theory

### 2.1 A generator is a prior predictive distribution

A ground-truth generator draws parameters and latent states θ ~ p(θ), then draws observables y ~ p(y | θ). This is the setting that simulation-based calibration (SBC) assumes. If the estimator's posterior p(θ | y) is computed correctly, the rank of the true θ among posterior draws is uniform [S: Talts et al. 2018; Cook, Gelman & Rubin 2006].

SBC has a blind spot. It cannot detect a posterior that simply returns the prior. Modrák et al. add data-dependent test quantities, and they call the joint data log-likelihood "an especially useful test quantity" [S].

For ADIC this makes the synthetic book more than test data. It is the **reference experiment** in which the framework's beliefs, actions and agents can be graded objectively [I].

### 2.2 Three layers: world, truth, observation

| Layer | Contents | Owner | Visible to estimators? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **World** | Regime path S_t ∈ {B, N, D, C} (stream 04); VIX label V_t ∈ {V0..V4}, thresholds 14.3 / 18.4 / 24.4 / 45 (C3); factor returns (equity, rates, credit, oil, FX); exit-window factor X_t; valuation gap z_t | Shared ESG (stream 10) | Yes: market data are public |
| **Truth** | Skill α_i (strategy, GP, vintage and idiosyncratic parts), economic NAV V_{i,t}, true cash flows, true conditional forward return μ_{i,t}, realized forward return ρ_{i,t→T}, defaults and recoveries | G9 generator | **No** (sealed) |
| **Observation** | Reported NAV N_{i,t}, cash-flow notices, the three return series (u, a, f), stated ranges, terms, bids and quotes, loan tapes, risk reports, overlay trades and CSAs | G9 bias layer | Yes |

This mirrors meteorology's *observing-system simulation experiments* (OSSEs) [S: Errico et al. 2013; Privé et al. 2013]. A "nature run" (the truth) is sampled by synthetic instruments with realistic error models, and the instruments are used to test data assimilation. The G9 observation layer plays the role of those instruments, and the stream-07 fusion engine plays the role of the assimilation scheme [I].

### 2.3 Conditional versus unconditional calibration

Published fund cross-sections, such as the Burgiss quantiles, are draws from one realized market history. Write the decomposition:

$$\mathrm{Var}(\ln TVPI)=\underbrace{\mathbb E_w[\mathrm{Var}(\ln TVPI\mid w)]}_{\text{skill, idiosyncratic, vintage within a world}}+\underbrace{\mathrm{Var}_w(\mathbb E[\ln TVPI\mid w])}_{\text{market-path uncertainty}}$$

Empirical dispersion estimates only the first term. The second term must be calibrated separately, to index-return distributions and capital-market assumptions (stream 10). Pooling simulated worlds and fitting the pooled quantiles to the data double-counts market risk. Prototype E1 shows the failure mode (§3.7) [I-SIM].

A related point: **TVPI is path-dependent and PME is transportable**. Calibrate skill and idiosyncratic risk to market-adjusted measures (PME or direct alpha), and let each simulated market path generate its own TVPI [I].

### 2.4 Identification and effective sample size

Forecaster bias is confounded with common shocks. When the forecast error is e_ij = b_g − δ_v + η_ij, the vintage shock δ_v is shared, so

$$n_{\text{eff}}=\frac{n}{1+(m-1)\,ICC}$$

where m is the number of resolved assets per vintage. With 16 vintages × 4 assets and ICC = 0.3, n_eff ≈ 34 out of 64 (stream 07's Example 5).

The generator must therefore emit both:
- **vintage and GP random effects**, so that estimators are tested on separating bias from common shocks;
- **regime-dependent bias**, which tests whether optimism is pro-cyclical [I].

### 2.6 Why structural simulation beats deep generative models when ground truth is needed

| Criterion | Structural (G9 core) | Deep tabular or time-series generators (CTGAN/TVAE, TabDDPM, GReaT, Quant GANs) |
|---|---|---|
| Needs real training data | No: calibrated to published moments | Yes. ADIC has none yet [S: method papers describe learning from data] |
| Known θ and truth columns | By construction | No interpretable θ; truth not defined |
| Counterfactual actions | Yes: SELL, HOLD, REFINANCE and LEVER re-run the same random numbers | No causal structure |
| Rare and regime stress | Controllable | Learns the training distribution; tails are thin with small n |
| Privacy | Nothing real to leak | Memorization risk; must be audited, e.g. the "authenticity" metric [S: Alaa et al.] |
| Realism of marginals | Only as good as the targets | Strong once data exist [S: TabDDPM reports superiority over GAN/VAE alternatives] |
| Role at ADIC | **Generator of record** | Realism challenger and nuisance-marginal filler once ADIC data exist |

### 3.2 Bootstrapping from public benchmarks

- **Public sleeves.** A stationary or regime-conditioned block bootstrap of index returns (stream 10) keeps real co-movement and skew. Use it for long-only benchmarks and hedge-fund factor returns.
- **Private sleeves.** Resampling published vintage quantiles (CA, MSCI) is useful only as a target generator. The quantiles come from one history and carry no path information [I].

### 3.3 Cross-sectional dependence

Use a factor structure plus a **grouped t-copula** for residuals:

$$\varepsilon_{i,t}=\sqrt{\nu/W_t}\left(\sqrt{\rho_g}\,\eta_{g,t}+\sqrt{1-\rho_g}\,\xi_{i,t}\right),\qquad W_t\sim\chi^2_\nu$$

- The common W_t produces tail dependence across all groups in stress.
- ρ_g is the within-group residual correlation. Default 0.10–0.20 [I].
- Vintage and GP random effects in α create the longer-lived dependence that the stream-07 calibration must handle.
- Holdings that share a GP (a PE fund and a co-investment alongside it) share the GP effect with correlation ρ_GP = 0.6 [I]. This gives stream 07's open question 7 a testbed.
- Vine copulas (Aas et al. 2009, via 10) are an optional upgrade for asymmetric tails [S].

### 3.4 Terminal-first quantile matching plus a bridge

To hit fund-level targets exactly while keeping quarterly structure:
1. Draw the fund's *terminal* idiosyncratic log-PME from a skewed, quantile-parameterized distribution fitted to the BGK quantiles. A metalog [S: Keelin 2016] or sinh-arcsinh fit works.
2. Simulate the systematic part along the world path.
3. Fill the idiosyncratic quarterly path with a Brownian (or t) bridge conditioned on the terminal value.
4. Run the call and distribution mechanics on the resulting economic NAV.

This fixes the skew mismatch in §3.7(c) while preserving identities and interim dynamics [I].

### 3.5 Realism checks

| Check | What it measures | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Stylized-fact tests (heavy tails, volatility clustering, leverage effect, absence of linear autocorrelation in liquid returns; Cont 2001 [S]) | Public sleeves and overlay drivers | Every release |
| Moment and quantile targets (§6.6) | Group engines | Every release; gate |
| Classifier two-sample test (C2ST): held-out accuracy near chance under the null [S: Lopez-Paz & Oquab] | Joint realism | Once ADIC data exist: train on synthetic vs real holdings features |
| MMD or energy distance (MMD: Gretton et al. 2012 [S]) | Distribution distance | Same |
| α-precision, β-recall, authenticity [S: Alaa et al.] | Fidelity, diversity, copying | Only if generative models are trained on ADIC data |

### 3.6 Validating estimators against the truth

1. **SBC on every Bayesian component** (fusion, hierarchical bias model, NAV nowcast). Include parameter and data-dependent test quantities, among them the joint log-likelihood [S: Modrák et al.].
2. **Parameter recovery.** Track bias, slope, noise and credibility weights against truth: RMSE, and coverage of 50/80/95% intervals, as a function of sample size and ICC.
3. **Held-out realized outcomes ("time travel").** Generate 2000–2040 synthetic histories, fit on data observable up to a cut-off, and score later realizations with PIT, CRPS and coverage.
4. **Decision regret.** Compare SELL/HOLD/REFINANCE/LEVER chosen from estimated beliefs with choices from oracle beliefs (true μ, true economic NAV) on common random numbers.
5. **Misspecification ensemble.** Score each estimator on generator variants it does *not* assume: heavier tails, regime-dependent optimism, nonlinear anchoring. Robustness is degradation under these variants, consistent with stream 10's structured-ambiguity logic [I].

### 4.2 Underwriting and marketing optimism (reference class)

**VC survey: 885 VCs at 681 firms [S: Gompers, Gornall, Kaplan & Strebulaev]:**
- The required IRR averages 31%; the required multiple has mean 5.5 and median 5.
- Funds market a net IRR averaging about 24% (median 20%) and a 3.5x multiple to LPs. That marketed IRR is similar to what PE firms market (per the authors).
- 93% expect to beat public markets.
- Only 1 in 10 quantitatively analyze their past decisions.

**Realized outcomes for comparison:** resolved-fund median net IRR is 11.3% for buyout and 3.5% for VC [S: BGK].

**Inference [I]:**
- The marketed-versus-realized gap at the median is about 9–13pp for buyout and about 17pp for VC.
- Part of that gap is definitional: required IRRs are hurdles or success-case figures, not expectations. ADIC's own IC underwriting is presumably haircut relative to GP marketing.
- I therefore set fund-level b^u at about +5pp, half the raw gap.

PE deal targets of about 22–25% gross, together with infrastructure and Green Book reference classes, are in stream 07 §4.1.

### 4.3 Reported-NAV behaviour

- **Fundraising inflation [S: BGK]:**
  - A typical distortion is about 2.6pp of quarterly return.
  - It reaches nearly 5pp for a fund 2 SD below peers after 5 years without a new fund, and about 1pp for a top performer.
  - Investors largely see through it: GPs who inflate rarely raise a next fund.
  - About 10% of top-tercile buyout and VC funds at life-end were bottom tercile at fundraising.
- **Bias by strategy [S: Jenkinson, Landsman, Rountree & Soonawalla 2020]:** 384 VC and 195 buyout funds, 1988–2016. Buyout NAVs show "little systematic bias". VC NAVs are "relatively aggressively biased", especially since 2000.
- **Conservatism at exit.** About 75% of exits clear the next-to-last mark [S, 04].
- **Smoothing and noise.** BGG smoothing and noise, and lagged quarterly returns (MSCI Q1/Q2-2026, buyout −1.1% / +2.5% against ACWI −2.7% / +15.1%), are in stream 04.
- **Tail-end funds** (10+ years) trade at discounts of 25% or more [S: Jefferies, via 04]. That gap is the basis for the tail-end over-mark term.

### 4.4 Forecaster behaviour

- **Anchoring [S: Campbell & Sharpe].** MMS consensus forecasts, 1990–2006: the typical forecast is weighted toward the previous release "on the order of 30 percent", with estimates of roughly 20–40% depending on the series.
- **Information rigidity [S: Coibion & Gorodnichenko, AER 2015].** Forecast errors are predictable from revisions, consistent with rigidity.
- **Overreaction vs underreaction [S: Bordalo, Gennaioli, Ma & Shleifer, AER 2020].** Individual forecasters overreact to their own news; consensus forecasts underreact.
- **Overconfidence.** Executives' 80% intervals cover 36% of outcomes [S: Ben-David, Graham & Harvey, via 07].
- **ADIC's own memo corpus.** Distribution timing was too optimistic after spread shocks and exit-window closures (context §2). This is the single most decision-relevant bias for liquidity.

### 4.5 Secondaries, co-investment, Capital Solutions

**Secondaries:**
- H1-2026 LP pricing (% of NAV): buyout about 91%, VC 79%, credit 89%, real estate 68%. Funds under 5 years old trade at single-digit discounts; tail-end funds at 25% or more. About 26% of deals include deferrals [S: Jefferies, via 04].
- Buyers out-earn sellers by about 5pp/yr in PME terms [S: Nadauld et al., via 04].
- Secondary funds settle into their final quartile in 5–7 years, against 6–8 for primaries [S: CA 2018, via 07].

**Co-investment deal dispersion [I].** No deal-level dataset could be verified this session. I therefore back the deal-level dispersion out of fund dispersion. A fund of n equally weighted deals with intra-fund outcome correlation ρ has

$$\sigma_{fund}=\sigma_{deal}\sqrt{\rho+(1-\rho)/n}$$

Solving with σ_fund = 0.40 (BGK) gives:

| Inputs | σ_deal(ln MOIC) | P(MOIC < 1), median MOIC 1.8x |
|---|---|---|
| n = 10–15, ρ = 0.1–0.3 | 0.66–1.00 | 19–28% |
| **Central case: n = 12, ρ = 0.2** | **0.77** | **22%** |

Evidence on adverse selection in co-investments is mixed in my memory: Fang, Ivashina & Lerner 2015 versus Braun, Jenkinson & Schemmerl 2020 (both †). I therefore default the adverse-selection term to 0 ± 2%/yr.

## 5. Practitioner and peer practice; case studies

1. **Bayesian workflow practice.** SBC is the successor to Cook–Gelman–Rubin posterior-quantile validation, which was designed to "establish the computational correctness of software" for Bayesian models [S]. The Stan community documents SBC as standard practice [S: Talts et al.].
   - ADIC lesson: every Bayesian engine in the lab (07 fusion, 04 nowcast, 10 regime model) ships with an SBC report on SYN-ADIC-177 before it touches ADIC data [I].
2. **Synthetic data in finance.** J.P. Morgan AI Research surveys synthetic-data uses across tabular, time-series and event data, with privacy, fairness and explainability motives [S: Potluru et al. 2023]. Open tooling exists, for example synthcity [S].
   - These efforts optimize *fidelity to real data*. G9's goal is different: *a known truth under a controlled data-generating process*. That is closer to López de Prado's case for backtesting on synthetic data (2018, ch. 13, "Backtesting on Synthetic Data") [S].
3. **Actuarial and meteorological analogues.**
   - Reserving methods are routinely tested on simulated triangles with known ultimates (†). For example, Gabrielli & Wüthrich (2018) publish a calibrated simulator of individual claims histories so that users can back-test their reserving methods [S].
   - Data-assimilation systems are tested by OSSEs (§2.2).
   - Both separate "nature" from "instrument", which is the G9 layering.
4. **LP pacing practice.** Takahashi–Alexander pacing is the de facto standard, and synthetic forward portfolios are routine (stream 04; Cliffwater for ERSRI).
   - G9 extends this in three ways: dispersion, the observation layer, and truth tables.
5. **Peer balance sheets as calibration anchors.** CPP shows how to report leverage on a notional basis (recourse leverage includes "the net notional value of derivatives used to generate additional leverage") and holds an explicit leverage limit [S].
   - The synthetic Treasury and Overlay tables should carry the same measures, so the IC sees peer-comparable ratios [I].
6. **Agent evaluation.** C4 phase 0 calls for "Synthetic data; contracts, registries and claim validator; cassettes; a 20–50-task evaluation suite".
   - Because truth is known, those tasks can have graded answers. Examples:
     - "flag the five most optimistic forecasts" (graded against bias draws);
     - "rank SELL candidates" (graded against oracle regret);
     - "explain the DPI shortfall" (graded against the regime path).
   - This turns Sakana-style rubric verification into objective scoring [I].

### 6.3 Ground-truth process

**(T1) World.**
- An HSMM regime path S_t, using the 04 transition matrix with duration dependence.
- Factors drawn from a multivariate t₅ conditional on S_t (stream-10 ESG).
- A mean-reverting valuation gap: z_{t+1} = φ z_t + σ_z ϵ_t, with φ = 0.95 per quarter, giving a stationary SD of about 0.2 [I].
- The exit-window factor X_t follows stream 04's E1.

**(T2) Skill hierarchy.**

$$\alpha_i=\alpha_{s}+a_{GP}+a_{v}+e_i,\quad a_{GP}\sim N(0,0.03^2),\ e_i\sim N(0,0.025^2),\ a_v=-0.5\,z_{v}+u_v$$

- Defaults for α_s: buyout +3%/yr, VC −1% with a t₃ right tail, growth +1%, secondaries +2%, co-investment +3% before the adverse-selection term [I-SIM/I].
- The total σ_α ≈ 4%/yr sits below Korteweg–Sorensen's firm-level spread (σ ≈ 6%) and above near-zero investable persistence.

**(T3) PE-type economic NAV (stream-04 RFT-TA):**

$$r_{i,t}=r_f\Delta+\beta_i(r^m_t-r_f\Delta)+\gamma_i\Delta cs_t+\alpha_i g(a^e)\Delta-\tfrac12\sigma_\varepsilon^2\Delta+\sigma_\varepsilon\sqrt\Delta\,\varepsilon_{i,t}+J_{i,t}$$

- Defaults: β = 1.0 (test 0.8–1.2); σ_ε = 0.19; ε ~ t₅; J ~ Bernoulli(0.0075) × N(−0.35, 0.20²) [I-SIM].
- Calls and distributions follow 04 E5/E6: the call schedule is 20/25/30/35/40% of remaining unfunded; distributions use L = 16, B = 2.0, with regime multipliers m^D and m^C and the effective-age clock κ(S).
- Secondaries use L = 8, B = 1.5.
- For exact targets, use the terminal-first variant (§3.4).

**(T4) Co-investment (company level).**
- Entry: EV₀ = M₀·EBITDA₀ with M₀ ~ the MSCI entry-multiple distribution (median 11.3x), and debt D₀ = ℓ·EBITDA₀ with ℓ ~ N(5.5, 1.0) [I].
- EBITDA growth g ~ N(0.06, 0.10²).
- Exit multiple M_T = M₀·exp(z_T − z_0 + ζ).
- Exit hazard has a median of 5.5y and is scaled by m^D(S).
- Default occurs if EBITDA/debt service falls below 1.
- Equity MOIC = (EV_T − D_T)/E₀. Calibrate so that σ(ln MOIC) ≈ 0.77 and P(MOIC < 1) ≈ 0.22.

**(T5) Capital Solutions.**
- Coupon c ~ U(9%, 14%) with a cash/PIK split.
- MOIC protection 1.3–1.5x; maturity 4–6y.
- Hazard λ = λ₀ exp(−κ_X X_t + κ_L LTV), with λ₀ = 2%.
- LGD ~ Beta, mean 0.45.
- The equity kicker is linked to the underlying PE NAV [I].

**(T6) Private credit (loan level).**
- Coupon: SOFR + spread, spread ~ N(575bp, 100bp), plus 1.5% OID.
- Rating chain with an annual PD of 2.5% × regime multiplier (N 1, D 1.5, C 3).
- LGD ~ Beta, mean 0.40.
- PIK toggles.
- Fund leverage of 0–1.25x where applicable.
- Long-run realized-loss target: 1.0%/yr [S].

**(T7) Hedge funds (monthly).**
- Returns: r = r_f + β′f + α + σε, with ε ~ t₄.
- Reported returns are smoothed by MA(2) weights θ from GLM, by strategy.
- Crowding jumps follow C3: intensity 0.5/yr in V3+ and size −1.5σ.
- Leverage (GAV/NAV) is drawn by strategy from Form PF.

**(T8) Long-only.** Returns: r = r_bench + a + TE·ε.

**(T9) Overlay.** Trades are priced on the VIX-anchored sticky-moneyness surface (09). VM and IM follow the CSA terms. Monetization rules follow C3.

### 6.4 Observation and bias layer (the three series)

**(O1) Reported NAV.** For PE-type assets:

$$r^N_{i,t}=(1-\lambda_s)r_{i,t-\ell}+\lambda_s r^N_{i,t-1}+\eta_{i,t}+\Delta m_{i,t}+\Delta c_{i,t}$$

- **Smoothing λ_q:** buyout 0.5; VC 0.6; private credit 0.3; Capital Solutions 0.4; co-investment 0.5.
- **Lag and noise:** ℓ = 1 quarter; η ~ N(0, 0.04²).
- **Manipulation:**

  $$\Delta m_{i,t}=\mathbf 1\{FR_{i,t}\}\min\!\big(0.05,\;0.013\,z^{gap}_{i,t}\,(1+0.2\,y^{noFund}_{i,t})\big)-0.25\,m_{i,t-1}\mathbf 1\{\neg FR_{i,t}\}$$

  This is calibrated to BGK's 2.6pp typical and about 5pp extreme distortions [I].
- **Conservatism and exit uplift:** ln(P/N_{t−2}) ~ N(0.10, 0.15²), so P(P > N_{t−2}) = 0.75. In regime C use μ_e = −0.05 [S anchor / I].
- **Tail-end over-mark:** +12% for assets aged over 10y.
- **VC over-mark:** +8% after age 6 [I].

**(O2) Underwritten u_i (at t₀, in the group-native metric).**

$$u_i=\bar\mu_{s,S_{t_0}}+R\,(\alpha_i-\alpha_s)+b^u_g+\gamma_u'x_i+\eta^u_i,\qquad R=0.3,\ \eta^u\sim N(0,0.03^2)$$

- x_i = {leverage > 6x EBITDA, multiple-expansion share > 50%}, each adding +2pp of optimism and fattening the left tail (memo corpus).

**(O3) Actual a_{i,t}.** Computed with ADIC's own code from observed cash flows and N: XIRR, TVPI, DPI, KS-PME, direct alpha. No extra noise is added; its bias is inherited from N.

**(O4) Forecast f_{i,t}.**

$$\tilde f_{i,t}=w_u u_i+w_a a_{i,t}+(1-w_u-w_a)f^*_{i,t}+b^f_g+b^{late}\mathbf 1\{a>8\},\qquad f_{i,t}=\rho_f f_{i,t-1}+(1-\rho_f)\tilde f_{i,t}+\eta^f_{i,t}$$

- f* is the oracle forecast given the group's information set; it is stored in `truth_bias`.
- **Timing optimism:** the forecaster's exit hazard equals the normal-regime hazard for k = 5 quarters after a high-yield spread shock above +150bp, or while S = D, then adjusts with a half-life of 4 quarters.
- **Overconfidence:** stated (q10, q90) = f ± 1.2816·κ_conf·σ_true.

### 6.7 Labeling and governance rules

- **L1 Names and IDs.** Dataset ID `SYN-ADIC-177-vX.Y`. Every ID carries a `SYN-` prefix. Fictitious names are drawn from neutral word lists, for example "Synthetic Harbor Partners IV". Never use real GP, company or ADIC identifiers.
- **L2 Field-level flags.** `is_synthetic=true` and `provenance ∈ {SYN, EXT, ADIC}` on every row.
- **L3 File-level metadata.** Parquet metadata `adic.synthetic=true`, plus generator version, seed and calibration-target hash.
- **L4 Visible marking.** Every chart, table and IC page carries the footer "SYNTHETIC – NOT ADIC DATA".
- **L5 Taint propagation.** Any output computed from at least one SYN input is SYN.
- **L6 Sealed truth tables.** Truth tables live in a separate store readable only by the validation harness. Agents and estimators never see them.
- **L7 Claim validation.** The C4 claim validator rejects any text that attributes SYN numbers to ADIC.
- **L8 Separation after real data arrive.** When ADIC data arrive, synthetic sets stay frozen and separate. Mixed runs are badged.

**Numerical pitfalls.**
1. **Within-world calibration.** Never fit cross-sectional targets to quantiles pooled across worlds (§2.3).
2. **Like-for-like targets.** Resolved-fund targets (BGK) are not comparable with all-fund samples (HJK). Censor and select consistently.
3. **Exact targets.** Use terminal-first plus bridge (§3.4) when exact targets are needed. Check that bridged paths respect NAV ≥ 0.
4. **Effective-age clock.** Keep it (04) to avoid mechanical distribution catch-up.
5. **Bitemporal integrity.** Forecasts may use only information with valid_time ≤ forecast date. Tests must fail on leakage.
6. **IRR.** Use bracketed XIRR. Report TVPI and PME alongside IRR.
7. **t-copula sampling.** Use one W_t per date shared across all holdings, otherwise there is no common tail dependence.
8. **Rescaling census draws** changes the size distribution slightly. Document it.
9. **Rare events.** Use importance sampling toward regime C and V3+, and report binomial standard errors (10).

## 8. Risks, pitfalls and limitations

- **Generator monoculture (Goodhart).** Estimators tuned to one generator can look excellent and fail on ADIC data.
  - Mitigation: a misspecification ensemble (§3.6, item 5) and freezing estimator code before scoring.
- **Target vintage mismatch.** BGK and HJK cover vintages about 1984–2008. CA covers 1995–2016 as of 2019. The 2022–26 drought, "12 is the new 5" and continuation-vehicle substitution may have changed dispersion and timing. Treat targets as priors and re-fit on ADIC data.
- **Unverified items.**
  - Deal-level co-investment dispersion is derived, not observed.
  - Long-only TE and IR cross-sections and credit-index volatility were not sourced.
  - Cliffwater figures come via a search summary.
  - The Open Protocol field list and SIMM CRIF details are from memory.
  - Fang–Ivashina–Lerner and Braun–Jenkinson–Schemmerl are cited from memory (†).
- **Bias parameters are design choices.** b^u, b^f, w and ρ_f are [I] defaults anchored on literature directions and magnitudes, not ADIC estimates. The point of G9 is to test whether ADIC's estimators can *recover* them, not to assert them.
- **Mislabeling or leakage risk.** Realistic synthetic numbers can migrate into IC materials. L1–L8 must be enforced in code: validators, CI checks and the claim validator.
- **False comfort from SBC.** SBC validates computation, not the model. A perfectly calibrated posterior under the generator can still be wrong about ADIC. Held-out realized tests on ADIC data remain decisive.
- **Privacy after real data arrive.** If generators are later fitted to ADIC data, apply memorization audits (authenticity metrics) before any external sharing.
