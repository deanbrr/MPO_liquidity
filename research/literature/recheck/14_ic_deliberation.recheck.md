**Method**

- **Limit.** For the three arXiv-only works (Michael, Laban, Wynn), "no peer-reviewed version" rests on the arXiv journal-ref and comment fields and on a Crossref title search (top 5 results).

**Verdict convention.** "ok", "mismatched" and "not found" describe metadata. Usage verdicts are given separately in the same cell.

## Summary

- **10 checked: 8 ok, 2 mismatched, 0 not found.** Every work exists. None is fabricated or garbled.
  - Fogliato: the dossier gives the arXiv author order for the FAccT paper, and omits pages and DOI.
  - Salvi: the dossier gives the preprint title for the journal version.
- **Usage problems (3)**
  1. **Fogliato et al. (2022).**
     - The participants were 19 *veterinary* radiologists.
     - Human-first ordering reduced agreement with the AI whether or not the AI was right, and brought no accuracy gain. The AI outperformed the participants, and the AI-first arm did marginally better (not significant).
     - The paper supports "reduces anchoring". It does not support "reduces uncritical agreement" or better decisions.
  2. **Salvi et al.** The dossier's +81.7% and N = 820 are arXiv v1 figures, and are labelled as such. The *Nature Human Behaviour* version reports +81.2% and N = 900.
  3. **ReConcile (minor).** "Up to 11.4%" is the gain over prior baselines. It is not an effect of model diversity.
- **Venue update.** Hackenburg et al. is now published in *Science* 390(6777):eaea3884 (4 Dec 2025), retitled "...conversational artificial intelligence". Its numbers are unchanged.
- **Clarifications (no number changes)**
  - Michael: state how consultancy is defined.
  - Khan: 48% and 60% are naive baselines.
  - Wynn: the degradation is not uniform.
  - Hansen: the §3 citation for restricting transcripts runs against the paper's net finding.
- **Every other attributed number matches its source:** Vaccaro, Khan, Michael, Laban, Wynn, Hackenburg and Hansen.
- **Against the companion file** (`research/verification/14_ic_deliberation.verify.md`, F4, F5, F7, F8, F9, F11, F13, F15, F16 and A6), all of its verdicts are reproduced. New in this re-check:
  - the FAccT author order for Fogliato, and the full-text accuracy result;
  - *NHB* volume, issue and pages for Salvi, and the *Science* issue date for Hackenburg;
  - the ReConcile wording point;
  - full-text support from Khan's and Wynn's own papers for the dossier's skill and weaker-agent claims;
  - the Hansen §3 note.

## Suggested wording for the dossier owner (not applied)

1. **Fogliato (§2.6).** "Veterinary radiologists (n = 19) who recorded a provisional decision first were 'less likely to agree with the AI regardless of whether the advice is accurate', at no time cost and with no accuracy gain (Fogliato et al. 2022)."
2. **Fogliato (§1, item 3(ii)).** Replace "human-first ordering reduces uncritical agreement with AI" with "human-first ordering reduces anchoring on AI output".
3. **Salvi (§2.5 and §4 row 9).** "Personalized GPT-4 produced an 81.2% relative increase in the odds of higher post-debate agreement compared with human opponents (N = 900; Salvi et al., *Nat. Hum. Behav.* 2025)."
4. **ReConcile (§2.5).** "ReConcile beat prior single- and multi-agent baselines by up to 11.4%; its component analysis found that 'the diversity originating from different models is critical to its superior performance'."
