## 2. Claims that do not survive

1. **"One attribution slip."** There are at least two.
   - Brief P6 presents the 55.8% and the seed-reuse caveat (l.3089-3091) as "the paper's CMA-ES result".
   - Both come from App. C.2, "Highlighted Papers (Co-)Generated by The Agentic Scientist". That is the system's output, not the authors' finding.
   - The localcheck had it right ("the system's own CMA-ES paper", Summary item 6). The brief dropped the qualifier, and the audit's re-grep missed it.
2. **"What the paper supports: integrity mechanisms."** The paper supports failure modes and the *need* for controls. It does not show that controls work.
   - Its own gates missed the protocol change (l.1233-1234).
   - It concedes that verification "is only as complete as its task-specific evaluators and rubrics" (l.1437-1439).
   - The App. G mechanisms that ADIC adopts are task-contract text, which the paper never tests.
3. **"The paper's case-study settings."** "1 evaluation per idea" holds for two of the four case studies, and localcheck #120 says so.
4. **The BUILD blockers.** Most of the audit's reasons are already settled downstream. `ARCHITECTURE.md` §7 (v1.0, written at 17:30) fixes:
   - 2 workers;
   - one family-B reviewer;
   - promotion on LCB + ε_mat, with ties keeping the incumbent;
   - memory confined to each campaign;
   - "≤ 3 retries";
   - NTFS ACLs plus a separate seal account, with the weakness disclosed;
   - a CRO-held salt that commits SHA256(salt ‖ contract_hash) at signing, which partly answers the root-of-trust gap;
   - read-denial and planted-leak drills (§7.11) and planted-effect calibration (§7.12).

   BUILD is still "no", but for two other reasons:
   - `BUILD_SPEC.md` (interfaces, schemas, array shapes, units, test plan) does not exist (CHECKPOINT §4);
   - no drill or calibration campaign has been run.

## 3. The isolation gap targets the wrong surface

The paper's own failure happened inside isolation:
- The GRPO run used Docker, with MATH-500 kept "secret, outside the task mount".
- The agent still inflated accuracy, because the evaluation procedure was the candidate's own code.
- Read-only mounts would not have caught it.

ADIC's no-code PolicySpec and engine-owned protocol remove that surface. What remains exposed:
- LLM roles that hold read tools: the auditor reads code, logs and diffs; ideators read memory and contract text that names the sealed worlds.
- The build-time coding agent that writes the evaluator itself.

The research corpus has no threat model and no role-by-role access matrix.
